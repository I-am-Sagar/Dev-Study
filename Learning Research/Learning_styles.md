### The Biggest Myth In Education _by Derek Muller_

People believe that there are learning styles - VARK. 
* **Visual:** People who prefer learning by seeing things. 
* **Auditory:** People who prefer learning by listening things. 
* **Reading/Writing:** People who prefer learning by reading and writing things. 
* **Kinesthetic:** People who prefer learning by doing things. 

Review articles and experiments on learning styles consistently conclude there is no credible evidence that learning styles exist. One of the reasons why people they have a learning style is because they already believe it is true. For example, most people believe that they are visual or kinesthetic learners. When a teacher shows them a diagram of working of a pump, and suddenly the concept clicks, they interpret as the evidence of learning styles. Where in reality, that diagram just be a great diagram that could have helped anyone learn. 

This is a very strong case of *confirmational bias*, when we already believe the world to be a certain way, then we interpret new experiences to fit with those beliefs - whether they actually do or not. 

#### So what actually improves learning?

There's a large body of literature that supports the claim that everyone learns better with multimodal approaches where words and pictures are presented together rather than either words or pictures alone. That's why videos can be such powerful tools for learning when the narration complements the visuals and have some text on the screen as well. 

Ultimately, the most important thing for learning is not the way the information is presented but is what happening inside the learner's head. People learn best when they are actively thinking about material, solving problems, or imagining what happens if different variables change. And the best part is - this strategy not only works for some learners, but eveyone.
